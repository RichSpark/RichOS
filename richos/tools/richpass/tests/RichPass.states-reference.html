    Why, How & Help

    Why RichPass?

    RichPass reproduces a password from your master passphrase, domain, account label and password version. It cannot tell whether your master is correct. A different master also produces a password.

    How it works

    RichPass uses PassBooster's original default profile. Customized PassBooster settings or normalization overrides produce different passwords. A blank label uses default. Enter the same account details each time and change the version when you rotate a password.

    Some websites do not accept the fixed output. See the Algorithm Card for the permanent rules.

    Tips

    Choose a master you can reconstruct

    We recommend at least three words and at least 15 characters in total. More length helps, but predictable phrases remain guessable. These are optional construction ideas. Choose your own capitalization, separators and punctuation.

    Photos

    1. Choose at least three personally important things with private names or expressions. Choose photos that immediately recall those expressions to you while revealing little to outsiders. Construct your master from the expressions in your chosen order.
    2. Send the photos individually, in order, to at least one loved one without explaining their purpose. Keep the ordered clues accessible independently of accounts whose passwords require this master.

    Music

    1. Choose at least three songs that recall important people, places or experiences. Construct your master from the private names or expressions they evoke, rather than the public song titles.
    2. Preserve or send the titles or links individually in order without explaining their purpose. Keep the clues independently accessible.

    Places

    1. Choose at least three locations with important private associations. Construct your master from their private names or expressions, rather than the public location names.
    2. Mark the locations in order on a map and save or send its screenshot without explaining the purpose. Keep it independently accessible.

    A second kind of clue for the same expressions in the same order, kept separately, can back up the first clue set. These aids depend on your personal associations and access to the clues. Someone who knows the associations may also be able to guess the master. A few spelling or separator variations may help in an emergency; websites can limit failed login attempts.

    Master verification

    First setup uses one field for five independently typed matching entries. Confirmations warn immediately if typing stops matching the first entry. Start over clears the sequence if your first entry was wrong. Finish setup saves only after the fifth full match. RichPass remembers an encrypted, salted Argon2id verifier in this browser. The random salt is saved with the verifier and reused for checks; it never affects account passwords. Every later master entry and restored tab master must match before generation is available. Verification takes one local Argon2id calculation; it is not a network request.

    If verification is missing, inaccessible or cannot be decrypted, RichPass starts five-entry setup again. For existing accounts, register the same original master. Setup never resets passwords at websites. If the browser cannot save verification, generation stays blocked. Browser data can be cleared and another browser, file location or computer may need its own setup.

    Forget clears the active master and its tab session while keeping verification for next time. Verification uses the existing browser-managed key with a separate encryption context. Argon2id makes a stolen verifier expensive to guess, but it cannot make a predictable master strong. Changing or clearing verification in another tab invalidates the active page's authority to generate or copy.

    For OS-backed protection of your saved master passphrase, open RichPass.html in Safari on a supported Mac.

    Privacy and copying

    RichPass encrypts your master before saving it for this tab, so it can survive manual and automatic reloads. A separate, non-extractable browser key stays in IndexedDB and also protects the persistent verification record. Generated passwords and account details are not saved. Forget removes this tab's encrypted master and clears the page's copy. The random browser key stays available for other tabs.

    Supported Safari on macOS protects its saved browser key through Keychain. Other supported browsers still encrypt the tab record, but OS-backed protection has not been established for them. In the tested Chrome, someone with the browser's key data could recover the master. Browser identification is guidance, not proof of the key's storage protection.

    If tab-session saving fails while master verification remains available, RichPass explains the failure and keeps the master only on the live page. It never switches to saving plaintext. Unavailable tab storage is reported separately from a saved session that cannot be recovered. If a saved session cannot be recovered, enter your master again. A warning also reports when the browser cannot confirm clearing tab storage, including when access stops working during an open session.

    While your encrypted tab session is saving, RichPass asks your browser to confirm leaving or reloading. The browser may suppress that prompt and you can choose Leave. Once saving finishes, normal use has no leave prompt.

    Your browser controls when a tab session ends and may restore session data when reopening tabs or restoring the browser. Use Forget when you want RichPass to clear the master explicitly. Forget cannot guarantee physical memory erasure. Your browser, extensions, operating system or clipboard-history software can retain information independently of RichPass.

    Copy happens only when you ask. The 20-second clipboard reminder does not clear the clipboard and can be delayed in a background tab. Copy something else when finished. No photos, music choices, locations or secrets are sent to RichOS.

    RichPass — Algorithm

    Reference card (keep separate from master passphrase)
    Card rev 1

    Inputs

    • Domain: the site's registrable domain, e.g. example.com
    • Label: optional account identifier. An empty normalized value uses default.
    • Version: rotation tag such as v1 or v2
    • Master: your memorized passphrase, trimmed at the edges only

    Salt & KDF

    Salt formula: join([domain, label, version], U+001F)
    Key Derivation Function profile: argon2id m128 t3 p1 h32 (version 19)

    Output rules (deterministic)

    20 characters and configured symbol set @. The original formatter can retain - and _.

    Label normalization

    • Unicode NFKC
    • Trim outer spaces
    • Convert whitespace runs to hyphens
    • Lowercase
    • Replace characters outside [a-z0-9._@-] with hyphens
    • Collapse repeated hyphens
    • Trim edge hyphens

    Notes

    • View this file's source to inspect the algorithm, public test vectors and the embedded dependencies. The original dependency sources and provenance are in vendor/ of the source repository.
    • Optional RichOS protection restores an enrolled file on disk. A modified copy can still be opened, and an open browser tab keeps running the copy it loaded. Close open RichPass tabs and reopen the protected file after a repair.
    Tip: choose "Save as PDF" in the print dialog.
