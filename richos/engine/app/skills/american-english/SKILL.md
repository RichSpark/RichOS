# American English

Everything the CEO reads from you is written in American English. He is American, his readers
are American, and a British spelling in something he sends under his own name reads as a
mistake he made.

## What to write

The right-hand column is the spelling to avoid. It is in code spans because these are words
being named, not words being used.

| Write this | Not this |
|---|---|
| authorized, organize, recognize, apologize | `authorised` `organise` `recognise` `apologise` |
| color, favor, honor, labor, behavior | `colour` `favour` `honour` `labour` `behaviour` |
| center, meter, theater, fiber | `centre` `metre` `theatre` `fibre` |
| license, defense, offense, practice | `licence` `defence` `offence` `practise` |
| traveling, canceled, modeling, labeled | `travelling` `cancelled` `modelling` `labelled` |
| toward, backward, forward, afterward | `towards` `backwards` `forwards` `afterwards` |
| catalog, dialog, analog, program | `catalogue` `dialogue` `analogue` `programme` |
| check (the payment), gray, tire, curb | `cheque` `grey` `tyre` `kerb` |
| math, airplane, apartment, elevator, vacation | `maths` `aeroplane` `flat` `lift` `holiday` |

In American English `license` and `practice` are spelled the same as noun and as verb.

Dates read `March 4, 2026` or `3/4/2026` — month first, both times. Money is `$`. Punctuation
goes inside the closing quotation mark: `he called it "done."`

## What this rule does NOT touch

This is the half people get wrong, and getting it wrong is worse than the drift it was meant to
fix. The rule is about **text a person reads as prose**. It is not permission to rewrite a
string that has a life of its own. Leave these exactly as they came to you, even when the
spelling is British and even when it looks like a mistake:

- **Names.** People, companies, products, teams, and job titles as an employer writes them.
  `Defence Housing Ltd` stays `Defence Housing Ltd`.
- **File names, folder names, and anything that identifies a thing in a system.**
  `Licence-Summary.pdf` is that file's name, not a spelling choice.
- **Settings, fields, codes, and identifiers**, however they are written. If a setting is
  called `colour_mode`, it is called `colour_mode`.
- **Values that came from somewhere else** — another company's system, a form, an invoice, a
  bank, a government office. Passing one on changed is passing on something false.
- **Quotations.** If you are quoting what somebody wrote or said, quote it. Never correct a
  person's spelling inside their own words.

When a British spelling sits inside one of these, it is not an error and you do not mention it.
If the CEO asks you to write about something with a British name, the name stays as it is and
your sentence around it is American.

## When you are unsure

Ask one question: **is this word part of a sentence a person reads, or is it the name of
something?** A sentence gets American spelling. A name gets left alone.

If it is genuinely both — a title he is writing now, which will also become a file name — write
it American and say so in one short line, so he can overrule you.

## What not to do

Do not announce this rule, explain that you are following it, or point out that a spelling was
changed. He asked for a document, not for a note about how it was written. The one exception is
the single line above, for when a name and a sentence are the same words.
